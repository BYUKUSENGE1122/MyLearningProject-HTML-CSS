Gorilla Trekking in Rwanda

An absolute beginner's comprehensive roadmap to visiting the majestic mountain gorillas in the wild.

What is Gorilla Trekking?

Mountain gorillas cannot survive in captivity or zoos. They only live wild in two isolated mountain regions in East Africa.

Trekking means you join a small group, hike into their jungle home with professional park rangers, and track them down based on where they slept the night before. Once you find them, you watch them play, groom, eat, and rest.

Where Does It Happen?

In Rwanda, gorilla trekking takes place exclusively in Volcanoes National Park (Parc National des Volcans).

  • Location: Northwest corner of Rwanda.
  • Landscape: A stunning chain of five sleeping volcanoes covered in dense rainforest and thick bamboo.
  • The Nearest Base: The vibrant town of Musanze, located right outside the park boundaries.

What to Expect on Trekking Day

The experience is structured, safe, and highly organized by park authorities.

07:00 AM

1. The Morning Briefing

You arrive at the Kinigi Park Headquarters early. Enjoy complimentary Rwandan coffee or tea while watching traditional dancers. Rangers divide visitors into small groups of exactly 8 people based on hiking fitness choice.

Hike Phase

2. The Forest Hike (1 to 6 Hours)

You drive to the trail starting point and begin tracking. Advance trackers go into the forest early to locate the gorillas. Your main guide stays in radio contact with them so you walk directly toward the family.

The Meeting

3. The Magical Hour (60 Minutes)

Once found, you leave bags and walking sticks with tracking porters. You step forward to spend exactly 1 hour watching them. You can take photos and videos, but flashes are strictly forbidden.

Essential Packing List

The weather in the mountains changes rapidly. Dress in layers to stay comfortable throughout the trek.

Gardening Gloves

Vital for grabbing wet branches, stinging nettles, and thorny bushes while climbing.

Hiking Boots

Waterproof shoes with deep grip patterns to prevent slipping on thick hillside mud.

Long Socks

Tuck your long pants directly into your socks to block safari ants from crawling up legs.

Rain Jacket

Lightweight rain gear is critical as rain can pour unexpectedly at any time.

Key Rules for Conservation

Because humans and gorillas share 98% of the same DNA, wild gorillas can easily catch human illnesses. Strict rules protect their health ecosystem.

Rule Command Why It Matters
Maintain 7m (23ft) Distance Keeps the gorillas relaxed and stops germs from airborne spreading.
Code Safe: Do Not Go Sick A simple human cold can be fatal to an entire mountain gorilla family.
Keep Voices Low Loud noises can frighten young infants or make silverbacks aggressive.
No Flash Photography Bright camera flashes startle wild animals and can trigger a defensive charge.
